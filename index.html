Simple printer mode For basic/entry-level FDM printers (larger nozzle, less precision). Caps mesh resolution, forces extra smoothing, raises minimum relief/base thickness, and turns off fine brushstroke texture — favoring wide, unambiguous plateaus a coarse nozzle can still reproduce, so the piece stays readable by touch even when fine detail can't survive the print.
Active: resolution capped at 80, smoothing ≥ 2, texture disabled, relief ≥ 2.5 mm, base ≥ 2.5 mm. Consider also raising "Detail levels" below to a few discrete steps — a handful of clearly separated plateaus tends to be more reliable to feel on a low-precision printer than a smooth continuous slope.

Before you print or hand this off: this tool gives a solid first pass, but good tactile graphics follow conventions this app doesn't fully know — like keeping raised lines simple, spaced apart, and testing legibility by touch before relying on it. The oil-painting mode leans on color-boundary detection and a light brushstroke texture, but it's still a best-effort translation of a visual medium — busy, highly detailed paintings will read more ambiguously by touch than simple, high-contrast ones. The braille output is uncontracted Grade 1 and a best-effort encoding (accented characters render as a blank cell); if this is for an actual blind reader, have them or someone trained in tactile graphics — e.g. the American Printing House — check a test print. No supports are needed for either model — both are flat-bottomed solids. If you're printing on a basic or entry-level FDM printer, turn on Simple printer mode — the primary goal is still a piece a blind reader can actually feel, even if that means a visually cruder, lower-resolution STL. If the artwork is larger than your printer's bed (or you have a small or tall, narrow build area), turn on Cut into pieces: you get one STL per piece plus an assembly guide, and the heights match exactly across every cut so the glued seam stays smooth under the fingers.
